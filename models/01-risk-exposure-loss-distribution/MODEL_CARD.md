# Model Card — Risk Exposure & Loss Distribution

## Model purpose

Quantify aggregate financial loss exposure from multiple uncertain event types and make expected loss, tail loss, reserve adequacy, stress behavior, and mitigation trade-offs understandable through an interactive interface.

## Intended use

- Demonstration of quantitative risk-management capability.
- Scenario analysis using synthetic or user-owned assumptions.
- Education on frequency/severity modeling and tail-risk metrics.
- Prototype decision support for reserve and mitigation discussions.

## Not intended for

- Regulatory capital reporting without independent validation and approved calibration.
- Automated insurance pricing or underwriting.
- Production credit decisions.
- Claims about a specific person, household, business, or organization's actual loss probability without appropriate data and calibration.

## Inputs

Each risk uses annual event frequency, typical event loss, large-but-plausible event loss, extreme-loss cap, severity distribution, frequency distribution, existing control effectiveness, and systemic sensitivity.

Scenario-level inputs include annual income / revenue, available reserve, risk-tolerance threshold, simulation count, and random seed.

## Outputs

- Expected / median aggregate loss
- Standard deviation
- VaR 95 / VaR 99
- Expected Shortfall 95 / 99
- Reserve and tolerance exceedance probabilities
- Confidence-based reserve requirement
- Expected-loss and conditional-tail contributions
- Stress comparisons
- Mitigation comparisons and annual control cost

## Core assumptions

1. Event severities within a risk are identically distributed conditional on scenario parameters.
2. Existing control effectiveness is modeled as a proportional reduction in event severity.
3. Selected mitigations may reduce frequency and/or severity by user-specified fractions.
4. Systemic dependence is represented by a shared mean-one frequency multiplier applied according to risk sensitivity.
5. Severity tails are capped at an explicit scenario-defined extreme loss.
6. Preset inputs are synthetic.

## Validation status

Automated tests cover numerical invariants, reproducibility, analytical-vs-simulation expected loss, attribution reconciliation, and monotonic stress / mitigation behavior. A 250,000-run convergence check is generated by `validation/validate_model.py`.

## Key model risks

- Parameter misspecification
- Inappropriate distribution selection
- Understated tail caps
- Dependence misspecification
- Overconfidence in mitigation-effectiveness assumptions
- Confusing scenario probabilities with objective forecasts

## Governance principle

The interface labels outputs as modeled results and keeps assumptions editable and visible. The model is designed to support human judgment rather than hide judgment behind a score.
